# Stakeholder Analysis

| Stakeholder | Role | Interest/Expectations | Influence on the System | How the System Affects the Stakeholder |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Club Officers/Presidents | Primary users who create events and submit budget requests on behalf of their club. | Want a fast, simple way to propose events and get budget approved without chasing advisors over email/WhatsApp; expect clear status on every request. | High — their day-to-day workflow directly shapes what features (event creation, budget forms) need to look like and how simple they need to be. | Saves time and reduces back-and-forth; but only if the system is genuinely easier than what they use now, otherwise they won't adopt it. |
| Club Members | Secondary users who view events and club announcements but don't create approval requests. | Want to easily see what events are happening and whether they're confirmed, without needing to ask officers directly. | Low/Medium — mainly influences the read-only/viewing features (event listing, dashboard) rather than core logic. | Gets clearer visibility into club activity; less relevant to them if the club rarely uses the approval features. |
| Faculty/Club Advisors | Approve or reject event and budget requests submitted by officers. | Want an efficient way to review requests, see relevant context (budget history, past events) before deciding, and not be overwhelmed with notifications. | High — the entire approval workflow is essentially built around what they need to make a decision, so their input matters a lot for that part of the design. | Reduces the amount of manual back-and-forth (emails, in-person sign-offs) but adds a new tool they need to learn and check regularly. |
| Student Council / University Administration | Potential secondary stakeholder interested in overall club activity and budget usage across the university. | Might want an aggregate view of how clubs are spending their allocated budgets, for oversight purposes. | Low at this stage — we're not building admin-level aggregate reporting in the current scope, so their influence is limited to a "nice to have" for now. | Could eventually get better visibility into club spending, but this isn't a guaranteed feature of the current version. |
| Development Team (us) | Designs, builds, tests, and maintains the system as a course project. | Want a project that's realistically achievable within one semester, demonstrates good software engineering practice, and results in a good grade. | High — we make all final decisions on scope, architecture, and what gets built in each increment. | Directly affected by how well we scope and manage the project; poor planning here creates more work for us later. |
